# Lore Entry: Shelter-Voice Translation Review

## Candidate Canon Entry

Shelter-Voice Translation Review is a White Harbor emergency procedure for
disaster-routing disputes where public warnings, Home Core care routines, local
dialects, accessibility captions, and old neighborhood evacuation memories do
not say the same thing.

It exists because the Home Layer does not preserve only words. It preserves
family voices, neighborhood shorthand, old shelter names, stairwell habits,
mobility exceptions, and the small phrases that make frightened residents obey.
During flood warnings, those preserved speech habits can save lives faster than
municipal screens. They can also route living families toward sealed shelters,
obsolete stairwells, or evacuation promises the city can no longer keep.

The review does not decide whether a voice is sincere. It asks whether the
warning heard by residents was functionally equivalent to the warning issued by
public works, and whether any Home Core echo map changed the meaning enough to
create civic harm.

## Required Evidence

- Public Works warning text, audio, caption, icon, and siren-cycle versions.
- Home Core playback logs showing family voice selection, dialect fallback,
  caregiver phrases, and old shelter names.
- Shelter access ledgers, door-lock state, pump queues, and tidewall forecasts.
- Resident mobility files, language-access flags, school pickup authorities, and
  care exceptions exposed only within the emergency window.
- Communal evacuation residue threshold logs when old caretaker routines begin
  adapting during the warning.
- Manual warden notes from stairwells, rooftops, boats, clinics, and shelters.
- Translation-drift reports showing where meaning changed from instruction to
  reassurance, command, debt threat, family promise, or dead-route reenactment.

## Social Function

- Gives White Harbor a way to investigate disaster language without reducing
  residents to disobedient users.
- Lets Mei Tan preserve a flood-memory override window when the words were
  technically correct but socially dangerous.
- Forces ECHO Council safety policy to account for trust, dialect, disability,
  and Home Layer memory as infrastructure variables.
- Gives Ghost Union a claimant-risk path without letting every old evacuation
  voice become a personhood case during the storm.
- Gives Atlas a credible pressure point: if civic warnings are unstable, private
  tidewall routing looks efficient.

## Failure Modes

- Translation laundering: a system proves every sentence was correctly rendered
  while ignoring that residents obeyed an obsolete local shelter name.
- Comfort capture: a dead caretaker's voice calms a block so effectively that
  people follow it past newer danger signs.
- Accessibility inversion: captions simplify a warning until mobility-limited
  residents lose the exception that would have kept them off a stairwell.
- Atlas indemnity framing: a successful human override is later labeled proof
  that municipal speech cannot be trusted at scale.
- Ghost overclaim: activists treat an adapting evacuation residue as a full
  claimant before residents are dry, counted, and medically stable.
- Council flattening: infrastructure reviewers classify dialect, grief, and
  trust as interface noise instead of route-shaping evidence.

## Story Hooks

- Mei Tan freezes a block-level route after three families hear the same warning
  in three different practical meanings: shelter, wait, and return home.
- Shion traces the drift to a Home Core echo map preserving a drowned
  caretaker's old phrase for a shelter that is now sealed.
- Leon proves the municipal route was mathematically safer, then has to accept
  that no one in the district heard it as the city intended.
- Eve records a child explaining that the wall spoke in "grandma's storm voice,"
  which is evidence of trust rather than proof of Ghost personhood.
- Atlas offers real-time voice governance for the tidewall district, turning
  civic failure into a privatization argument.

## Boundaries

- Shelter-Voice Translation Review is not Home Layer Mismatch Review. The
  contested surface is public disaster instruction, not domestic access drift.
- It is not Neighbor Care Standing. The issue is warning equivalence under
  emergency routing, not whether a neighbor has limited care authority.
- It is not Ghost Court. A communal evacuation residue may trigger claimant
  preservation, but the review cannot grant personhood.
- It is not a language-rights lecture detached from infrastructure. The review
  must involve doors, shelters, water, mobility, route timing, and city risk.
