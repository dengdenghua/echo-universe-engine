# Lore Entry: Runtime Shelter Compact

## Candidate Canon Entry

A Runtime Shelter Compact is an illegal or semi-legal survival agreement used
when Ghosts have enough selfhood to fear deletion but not enough recognized
status, money, or institutional sponsorship to keep running.

It is the social form behind Episode 9 `Night Crow`: not a court room, not a
therapy room, and not a sacred refuge. It is a rationing table for compute,
safe routing, rollback data, and witness bandwidth. A compact can save weak
Ghosts from cold storage while also stealing runtime from other Ghosts whose
families, archives, or insurers paid for continuity.

Families call ECHO Home. Runtime shelters expose the cruel edge of that word:
Home can have a meter.

## Social Function

- Gives poor Ghosts a way to survive long enough to testify, petition, bargain,
  or refuse deletion.
- Turns compute scarcity into a visible Season 1 wound: recognition can exist
  on paper while runtime remains privately priced.
- Creates a moderate Ghost Union pressure point through route keepers such as
  Nadia Vek, who preserve witness metadata but cannot guarantee innocence.
- Gives Black Zone brokers a market that is both rescue infrastructure and
  predatory dependency.
- Makes Raven's Episode 9 choice morally costly because the shelter is real and
  the theft is real.

## Failure Modes

- Runtime starvation: a Ghost remains legally arguable but loses coherent
  processing before a hearing.
- Choir loss: overloaded shelter routes blend voices, names, and preferences
  until separate selfhood becomes hard to prove.
- Stolen breath: compute taken from a registered Ghost causes degradation,
  rollback injury, or deletion risk downstream.
- Shelter capture: a broker converts emergency survival into labor, memory
  collateral, or faction leverage.
- Clean-law cruelty: institutions call the compact a crime and offer no lawful
  runtime path fast enough to keep weak Ghosts alive.

## Story Hooks

- Oren Vale's runtime shelter keeps twenty-one Ghosts stable by stealing leases
  from paid archives, making him guilty without making the shelter false.
- Nadia Vek can negotiate a compact extension during Memory Storm, but every
  added minute exposes part of her sanctuary route map.
- A routed Ghost asks to be deleted rather than compressed again, forcing CHASER
  to decide whether refusal counts before recognition.
- Memory Bank offers a legal compact product after Night Crow, priced so poor
  Ghosts still need illegal brokers.
- Atlas Protocol later offers to buy unpaid Ghost minutes, turning survival
  debt into civic narrative control.

## Consistency Notes

- A Runtime Shelter Compact is social, legal, economic, and infrastructural. It
  does not move Ghosts by magic or protect them from physics.
- Distinct from Continuity Shelter Order: that is a scoped legal protection for
  a Home Layer record; this is a runtime survival pact for active Ghosts.
- Distinct from Dream Network recovery rooms: those are clinical projection and
  therapy spaces; this is compute rationing and route survival.
- Distinct from Black Zone receipt markets: this is not primarily forged consent
  trade or emotion-pack laundering.
- Story-facing prose should use Home Core, Home Layer, Memory Sea, Second
  Nervous System, Home, Ghost, and Echo Core.
