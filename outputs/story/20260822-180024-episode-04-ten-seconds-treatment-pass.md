## Treatment Goal

Carry the revised Episode 3 ending into Episode 4 without revealing Project E-01
too early.

Episode 3 now ends on a narrow receipt field:

```text
stable transfer window: ten seconds
```

Episode 4 should make that field hurt Kane before it explains what the larger
buyer wants. The audience understands that someone is testing White Ghost Team
procedural tolerance. They do not yet receive Zero's sealed project name, ninth
upload status, Omega framing, Mother signal, or God Fragment context.

## World-First Opening

The episode opens with Tomas Vale, not Kane.

Tomas sits in a veterans' benefits hall with a paper cup cooling between his
hands. The room is gentle by design: soft civic light, framed service badges,
quiet chairs that do not scrape the floor, and a wall display promising that the
city remembers every defender.

His Home Core subsidy timer sits in the corner of his retinal overlay. It is not
a dramatic countdown for him. It is rent, medicine, and the last care routine
that lets his spouse recognize morning.

The clerk asks whether he can verify "essential continuity need."

Tomas begins to answer.

His right hand salutes before he tells it to move.

## Four-Act Treatment

### Act 1: The Room That Thanks Him

Tomas enters the benefits hall as a claimant. The civic system frames him as a
person owed gratitude until his file becomes difficult.

Key beats:

- The clerk asks for proof that the spouse's care routines remain "essential."
- Tomas tries to describe a morning phrase his spouse still recognizes in the Home Layer.
- His hand salutes without consent.
- The first guard falls from a movement Tomas remembers seeing in war but never learned.
- The hearing chamber enters veteran-safety lockdown, trapping civilians with him.

Act turn:

Eve, arriving with Kane and Shion, hears panic under the attack rhythm.

### Act 2: The Update Was Clean

CHASER Central prepares to classify the event as body occupation with lethal
containment permission. Kane refuses a clean shot because Eve confirms Tomas is
still awake inside the motor overwrite.

Key beats:

- Shion finds the routine entered as a rehabilitation calibration patch.
- The patch vendor is legally contracted and carries a clean evidence watermark.
- The same shell route that bought Episode 3's receipt appears in a timestamp
  mismatch, not in a readable confession.
- Leon predicts the lock doors will turn the hall into a pressure trap if the
  occupied body keeps striking the same glass seam.

Midpoint reversal:

The combat routine belongs to Ilya Sorn, officially dead for nine years. Ilya is
not confirmed as a whole Ghost. The evidence supports a damaged procedural
package with fear residue and unresolved command cadence.

### Act 3: Ten Seconds

Kane can counter the routine, but only by opening himself to the same procedural
memory channel being used against Tomas.

He asks for ten seconds.

The ten seconds are not heroic spectacle. They are infrastructure vulnerability
made visible:

- Leon buys three seconds by predicting which door support will fail first.
- Eve buys three seconds by speaking Tomas's spouse's morning phrase through the
  hall speakers, anchoring Tomas against the overwrite.
- Zero buys four seconds by delaying CHASER's lethal authorization and ordering
  the room's Home Core emergency relays to stay live.
- Shion keeps the forged patch from refreshing mid-sync.

Kane receives the counter-drill as motion first: heel pressure, elbow angle,
sightline discipline, nonlethal disabling route.

Then Ilya's final fear arrives.

Ilya died under autonomous medical glass while command kept requesting mission
continuity. The residue is not a soul returning from death. It is an active
pattern of duty, fear, habit, and obedience preserved until it can move again.

Kane wins without killing Tomas.

He cannot give the fear back.

### Act 4: Useful Residue

The system wants a tidy classification:

```text
claimant: Tomas Vale
incident class: contaminated violence risk
vendor status: under review
combat package: obsolete training residue
public risk: contained
```

Kane refuses the violence-risk label and logs the event as unauthorized
procedural occupation through a care-status channel.

Tomas asks whether that will keep his spouse's subsidy active.

No one can answer fast enough.

Shion shows Kane the final receipt match. It contains no project label and no
origin file, only a buyer-side evaluation:

```text
procedural recipient profile: restricted
stable transfer window: ten seconds
foreign-load residue retention: positive
care-channel exploit: confirmed
```

Kane understands the test was built around him. Zero understands the buyer
avoided naming what it was really measuring.

Final line option:

Kane says, "If the city can keep using Ilya after death, it can do the same to
any of us."

Zero answers, "Only if we let the record call it useful."
