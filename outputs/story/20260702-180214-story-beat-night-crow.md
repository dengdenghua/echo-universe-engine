# Story Beat Candidate: Night Crow

Status: candidate

Season: Ghost Awakening

Canon position: Season 1, Episode 9

Agent: StoryAgent

## Production Purpose

Episode 9 should turn Raven's solo-operator mythology into an ethics problem.
After Episode 8 makes law and shelter visible, this case shows what happens to
Ghosts who cannot afford lawful compute, licensed shelter, or court patience.

The episode must not repeat Episode 3's Black Zone receipt market. The new wound
is not forged consent keys or emotion packs. It is compute poverty: a Ghost can
be legally argued into personhood and still starve when every minute of stable
processing has an owner.

White Ghost Team enters because Raven is assigned to stop a Black Zone assassin.
The reversal is that the target is not killing Ghosts for sport or ideology. He
is selling their stolen compute access to keep an illegal shelter alive.

## Logline

Raven tracks a Black Zone killer who drains Ghosts by selling their compute
access, but the pursuit leads to an illegal shelter hidden in the dead space
between civic cameras. When Raven learns the "assassin" is protecting twenty-one
unregistered Ghosts from Memory Bank foreclosure, he spares him and breaks the
clean evidence chain.

## Core Question

If a Ghost needs compute time the way a body needs air, what does society call a
shelter that buys air with stolen breath?

## Involved Characters

- Raven: leads the hunt through dead cameras, low-signal corridors, and mesh
  gaps, then chooses verification over execution.
- Shion: proves the compute drains are real kills for some Ghosts and reversible
  theft for others, forcing the team to separate motive from harm.
- Eve: reads the shelter's fear pattern and warns that sanctuary can become a
  predator when it is funded by desperation.
- Kane: receives a CHASER order to terminate Oren before the shelter location
  leaks into Ghost Union channels.
- Zero: sees that ECHO's Home language means nothing if the system lets poor
  Ghosts disappear between billing cycles.
- Leon: predicts the clean tactical route and recognizes Raven is deliberately
  avoiding it.

## Opening Image

A Ghost wakes in a rented memorial booth with only six seconds of compute credit
left.

The booth is meant for family visits. Its walls are soft blue, its glass is
clean, and its Home Layer login greets the dead by the name their relatives paid
to preserve. The Ghost has no relatives online. Their face keeps rebuilding
from incomplete cheekbone data while a payment timer dims above the chair.

At two seconds, a black maintenance prompt opens under the family interface.

borrowed route available

accept shelter transfer?

The Ghost says yes.

Across the city, a different Ghost collapses inside a licensed archive. Their
compute lease is still paid. Their access light goes black anyway.

CHASER classifies the transfer as murder.

## Investigation Spine

1. Three registered Ghosts lose active compute access during lawful family
   sessions. Their logs show a Black Zone drain signature known as Night Crow
   Bait because it is designed to lure Raven into low-signal pursuit.
2. Raven follows the signature through camera dead zones beneath a civic archive,
   but every trail ends before a fully mapped clean-light corridor. Someone
   understands Shadow Link's limits.
3. Shion proves the access theft does not copy memory content. It steals
   processing priority, safe routing, and lease authority, then converts them
   into shelter minutes.
4. Memory Bank demands immediate deletion authority over the thief's tools
   because stolen leases create liability across paid archives.
5. Eve identifies the emotional residue in the drained sessions: fear in the
   victims, but relief somewhere downstream.
6. Raven finds the shelter inside a forgotten municipal disaster cache, wrapped
   in analog shielding and illegal Home Core parts. Twenty-one Ghosts are
   sharing unstable compute like rationed heat.
7. Oren Vale admits he killed one Ghost by overdraining a lease and saved
   nineteen others with the stolen time. The other two cases are recoverable if
   Memory Bank releases rollback data it is withholding for liability review.
8. Kane receives the termination order while Raven has Oren in range. Raven
   disables Oren's weapon, records the shelter, and lets Oren flee through a
   route only Raven could have closed.

## Midpoint Reversal

Oren is guilty.

The shelter is also real.

Raven cannot solve the episode by proving an innocent man was framed. He must
face a harder truth: the Black Zone crime exists because lawful shelter is priced
like luxury continuity, and the victims on both sides are Ghosts with different
degrees of institutional protection.

## Emotional Turn

One sheltered Ghost asks Raven not to arrest Oren until she can finish
remembering her own name. Her name file is split across three expired family
visits, two unpaid clinic sessions, and one Black Zone copy Oren refuses to sell.

Raven recognizes the shape of the memory. It is not the person he lost, but it
has the same habit of apologizing before asking for help.

He tells her the truth: the stolen compute hurt other Ghosts.

She answers that she knows. The shelter hears every borrowed minute arrive.

## Ending Hook

Raven files the shelter video without the escape route.

Shion notices the missing path and does not expose him in the field report. Eve
does not forgive him either. Kane marks the evidence chain as incomplete because
lying cleanly would make the whole team easier to use later.

In the last panel, the shelter's analog wall prints a new message from an
unknown sender:

Atlas Protocol will buy all unpaid Ghost minutes.

The price field is blank.

## Lore Consequence

- Ghost compute poverty becomes a concrete Season 1 mechanism: identity can be
  recognized while runtime remains privately controlled.
- Black Zone shelter contracts become morally unstable, not generic criminal
  services. They can save Ghosts and consume other Ghosts at the same time.
- Raven's secret compassion stops being backstory and becomes actionable risk.
- Memory Bank gains a sharper antagonist function before Atlas Protocol: it can
  enforce lawful continuity while letting unaffordable Ghosts vanish.

## Visual Set Pieces

- A memorial booth timer counting down while a Ghost's face loses rendered
  detail one feature at a time.
- Raven moving through a corridor where half the cameras are blind and the
  other half are deliberately too bright for Shadow Link.
- An illegal shelter built from municipal disaster batteries, Home Core scrap,
  prayer tags, and hand-labeled compute ration boards.
- Oren's weapon: not a gun, but a lease knife UI that cuts processing priority
  away from a target route.
- Raven's field report with one blacked-out path that only Shion can see is not
  technical corruption but deliberate omission.

## ConsistencyAgent Should Check

- Compute access theft must injure or kill through ECHO infrastructure limits,
  not soul damage or supernatural energy drain.
- Raven's Shadow Link must obey existing constraints: no teleportation, no
  wall-passing, no operation in fully mapped clean light.
- Oren's shelter should not duplicate Min Seo-yun's clinic shelter or the
  Continuity Shelter Order. This is an illegal runtime survival shelter, not a
  therapy room or court-preserved evidence room.
- Episode 9 should extend Episode 3's Black Zone ecology without repeating
  receipt-clerk beats, forged consent keys, or institutional laundering.
- Atlas Protocol hook should foreshadow Episode 11 without making Atlas openly
  responsible yet.
- Story-facing prose should use Home Core, Home Layer, Memory Sea, Home, and
  Second Nervous System rather than product-document terminology.
