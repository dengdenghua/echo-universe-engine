## Purpose

Episode 9 needs Raven's solo-operator myth to become a team trust problem.
Night Crow should not make Raven look clean because he shows mercy. His choice
protects twenty-one unregistered Ghosts, preserves an illegal shelter long
enough to matter, and also breaks the evidence chain around real compute harm.

This update records the relationship pressure created when the target is both a
thief and a shelter keeper, and when lawful continuity cannot keep poor Ghosts
running between hearings.

## Relationship Logic

- Oren must remain guilty. He overdrained one Ghost, endangered two recoverable
  cases, and made the shelter survive by consuming other Ghosts' paid runtime.
- Raven's mercy should create debt, not absolution. He records the shelter and
  disables Oren's weapon, but the missing escape route makes Shion, Kane, and
  Leon reassess how much of Raven's field judgment is private law.
- Shion becomes the technical holder of the breach. She can prove the omission
  and the compute theft path, yet keeps the report intact because exposing Raven
  immediately would also expose the shelter before rollback data is secured.
- Kane carries command pressure rather than simple disapproval. He knows the
  termination order was institutionally clean, and he also knows clean execution
  would have erased twenty-one unregistered witnesses.
- Eve gives the case its emotional limit: sanctuary built on stolen runtime can
  become predatory even when the protected Ghosts are real people.
- Zero should not solve the case. He turns the phrase Home into an accusation
  against the system: a personhood ruling means little when runtime remains
  priced like luxury continuity.
- Memory Bank should become sharper but not melodramatic. Its rollback
  withholding is a liability tactic, and its cruelty comes from treating unpaid
  runtime as an accounting risk before a survival condition.

## Season 1 Use

Night Crow should carry forward into Episodes 10 and 11 as a trust fracture and
a faction bridge:

- Raven enters Shion's Episode 10 focus already exposed to a technical witness
  who can choose when to make his omission official.
- Kane's later mutiny path gains an early rehearsal: obeying a valid order can
  still destroy the only evidence that poor Ghosts are alive.
- Leon's rivalry with Raven gains moral content. Leon can read the clean route
  Raven avoided, but cannot reduce the choice to cowardice or vanity.
- Memory Bank, Ghost Union, and Black Zone shelter networks become connected by
  runtime scarcity rather than only by ideology.
- The Atlas Protocol hook should remain a price offer, not a reveal of direct
  Atlas control.

## Continuity Limits

- Do not retcon compute theft into harmless redistribution. It injures and can
  kill through priority, lease authority, safe routing, rollback access, and
  runtime starvation inside ECHO infrastructure.
- Do not let Raven's Shadow Link exceed canon constraints. He can use
  low-signal corridors, sensor shadows, machine blind spots, and mesh gaps; he
  cannot teleport, cross true signal voids, or bypass fully mapped clean light.
- Do not make Oren innocent, secretly CHASER, or a pure martyr. His protection
  works because he commits real harm.
- Do not make Shion's silence permanent forgiveness. It is a field containment
  choice while rollback data and shelter exposure remain unresolved.
- Do not promote this relationship web to canon until FactionAgent reviews the
  Memory Bank, Ghost Union, and Black Zone shelter economy and TechnologyAgent
  reviews the compute lease mechanism.
