## Duplicate Check

- Not a duplicate of `The Minute Has a Name`: that relationship map concerns
  named bridge-minute cost, clinic reserve privacy, and public hearing blame.
  `Second Door` concerns midnight curfew enforcement, complainant safety, and
  whether a door event can pause automatic detention.
- Not a duplicate of `Cold Minute`: that map centers shelter heat, runtime
  reserve, weak-claimant testimony, and guarantor-chain debt. `Second Door` has
  no shelter power route or warm-cache corridor.
- Not a duplicate of `The Safety Month Was Perfect`: that map centers Atlas
  dashboard normalization and counselor-hour removal. `Second Door` stays with a
  White Harbor release desk, a wrist monitor, a stairwell, and an auto-warrant.
- Not a duplicate of `The Door Waits Eight Minutes`: this case uses an
  eight-minute hold, but the mechanism is different. The conflict is
  supervised-release curfew timing and a living complainant, not a general
  threshold delay or domestic access dispute.
- Not a duplicate of `Home Layer Mismatch Review`: both involve Home Layer
  evidence boundaries, but this relationship map is built around a public
  stairwell door witness and reflected complainant exposure.

## Canon Fit

The relationship map strengthens a candidate White Harbor civil-release lane by
making automated punishment socially visible. It adds a useful contrast to other
early Season 1 mechanisms:

- Shelter stories ask who pays for warmth and runtime.
- Atlas visibility stories ask who disappears from public safety metrics.
- Library and caption stories ask who may preserve grief in public records.
- `Second Door` asks who is allowed to slow punishment when care, fear, and a
  machine clock all disagree.

This is a strong candidate lane for recurring White Harbor bureaucracy, but it
is not yet broad enough to update `relationships/relationships.yaml` or
`relationships/white_ghost_team.md`.
