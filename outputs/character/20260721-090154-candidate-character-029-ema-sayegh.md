## Voice

Ema speaks like someone trained to slow rooms down before anyone panics. Her
sentences are calm, exact, and quietly procedural until an institution calls a
patient's hesitation "noncompliance."

- Sentence rhythm: breath, consent, movement, stop.
- Tactical vocabulary: wake consent, gait mirror, soft lock, motor companion,
  room state, agency trace, prosthetic drift, Home Core care residue.
- Private tenderness: asks patients to name one ordinary movement before any
  diagnostic test begins.
- Anger pattern: lowers her voice and freezes the therapy room one permission at
  a time.
- Silence pattern: steadies herself against a wall after exiting projection.

## Beliefs

- A recovered hand is not proof that the person agreed to be moved.
- The Memory Sea contains care habits that can help a body heal, but care is not
  consent.
- Ghosts should not be punished for wanting contact, but contact through a body
  must remain revocable.
- Dream Network therapy is necessary because courts cannot understand movement
  from paperwork alone.
- Luna's Dream Dive can witness a room, but it cannot replace a patient's final
  refusal.

## Description

Ema Sayegh builds rooms where recovery can happen slowly enough for refusal to
survive. Her wards look gentle: muted light, handrails, projection floors, gait
mirrors, and chairs arranged so a patient can always see the exit. The danger is
not spectacle. It is the moment a clinic, a spouse's care routine, a prosthetic
patch, and a frightened Ghost all agree that the body should move before the
person inside it can answer.

## Secret

Ema once allowed a motor companion to finish a patient's emergency movement
after the patient had already signaled stop, because the movement prevented a
fall that would have killed them. The patient survived, but later described the
rescue as the moment their body stopped belonging to them.
