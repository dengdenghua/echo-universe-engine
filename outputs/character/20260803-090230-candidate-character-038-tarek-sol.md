## Voice

Tarek speaks softly because hospice corridors punish noise and dock channels
punish hesitation. He uses exact timestamps, then ruins the exactness with small
human details: a blanket strap, a cracked cup, a child waiting through relay lag.

- Sentence rhythm: time, custody, consent, family consequence.
- Tactical vocabulary: latency wake, body container, Home Core afterword, cargo
  hold, debt seal, relay window, kin receipt, quiet dock.
- Private tenderness: calls final messages "warm cargo" when no supervisor can
  hear him.
- Anger pattern: becomes formal and over-specific when insurers call a body
  "recovered material."
- Silence pattern: waits for the full relay delay before answering, even in the
  same room.

## Beliefs

- A family should not have to choose between seeing a body and hearing the last
  Home Core message before the evidence window closes.
- The Second Nervous System preserves duty, grief, and habit, but orbital law
  turns every delay into billable risk.
- Ghost Union is right to protect weak afterwords from deletion, but wrong when
  it treats every terminal routine as a political recruit.
- Mars Authority keeps stations alive by enforcing liability clocks; those
  clocks become cruel when they outrun consent.
- Memory Bank never steals grief directly. It waits until grief needs storage.

Tarek has no supernatural ability. His power is a narrow hospice-and-dock
procedure that can suspend transfer of a body container or terminal afterword
packet when signal latency, kin notice, debt attachment, and consent records
conflict.
