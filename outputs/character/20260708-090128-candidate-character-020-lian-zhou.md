## Voice

Lian speaks in calm diagnostic language because panic changes outcomes in her
rooms. Her kindness is procedural, almost severe: she offers water, names rights,
and corrects threat labels before she offers comfort.

- Sentence rhythm: short intake questions followed by exact summaries.
- Tactical vocabulary: exposure class, consent anchor, carrier risk, welfare hold,
  witness quarantine, pattern drift, exit criteria.
- Private tenderness: remembers names even when the form allows only case numbers.
- Anger pattern: quiet refusal to sign a label she cannot defend.
- Silence pattern: pauses before saying "not yet," because every delay becomes her
  liability.

## Beliefs

- Containment without exit criteria is punishment in medical clothing.
- A person carrying someone else's fear is not automatically someone else's weapon.
- CHASER is necessary because the Memory Sea can hurt people at scale.
- CHASER becomes dangerous when it confuses administrative speed with public safety.
- A Home Core witness should never be opened wider than the emergency requires.

## Fears

- That one delayed quarantine will let contamination spread through a hospital,
  school, or shelter route.
- That one fast quarantine will erase a real person before anyone proves the
  pattern was self-updating.
- That Zero's unresolved continuity will be classified by the same criteria Lian
  helped write.
- That public fear will make "patient" and "carrier" indistinguishable in CHASER
  language.

## Ability / Institutional Power

### Exposure Class Override

Lian has no supernatural perception, no combat specialty, and no hidden hacking
ability. Her power is a narrow CHASER classification authority: she can attach an
override note to a contamination case before it is locked into threat handling.

## Limitations

- No field command authority over White Ghost Team.
- Cannot inspect raw memories without consent, warrant, or emergency medical order.
- Cannot block a CHASER Central lethal containment order once emergency command
  protocol is active.
- Cannot distinguish trauma echo, forged signature, and emerging Ghost selfhood
  without external technical review.
- Cannot promise release; she can only define conditions under which release is
  legally possible.

## Description

Lian Zhou sits between CHASER's fear of civic collapse and ordinary civilians who
enter a triage room with someone else's memories in their bodies. She does not
deny that personality contamination can kill. She has seen exposure chains jump
from a Home Core grief loop into a clinic routine, then into a school access path
before anyone understood the route. But she also knows the first label changes
everything: patient, witness, carrier, threat.

Her character pressure is not rebellion. It is classification discipline under
public terror. In Episode 12, as Memory Storm spreads across several cities, Lian
becomes the official who can keep exposed people from disappearing into permanent
threat detention for a few more hours. That mercy is not pure. If she is wrong,
the delay can injure strangers. If she is right, the delay may be the only reason
a frightened person is not erased before anyone proves they were still themselves.

## Secret

Lian helped write an early draft of the continuity-risk rubric that can later be
used to justify detaining Zero. She did not design it for Zero, but she recognizes
her own language when the form begins turning unresolved identity into executable
containment.

## Canon Risks

- Do not portray Lian as anti-CHASER by default; she believes containment is
  necessary.
- Do not make her mercy always correct. Her authority should create real risk.
- Do not let her override lethal containment during Episode 15 without separate
  canon approval.
- Do not turn exposure classification into supernatural diagnosis. It is a
  fallible institutional workflow using ECHO infrastructure evidence.
- Do not use product-document terms in prose scenes; use Home Core, Home Layer,
  Memory Sea, Second Nervous System, or ECHO.
