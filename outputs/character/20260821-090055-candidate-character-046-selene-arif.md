## Voice

Selene speaks like a supervisor who has to keep people out of dangerous rooms
while knowing some rooms are evidence. She is plain, dry, and exact about what
must be bagged, held, or left alone.

- Sentence rhythm: room, order, hazard, hold, witness.
- Tactical vocabulary: reset order, hold receipt, live-care dependency,
  disconnect notice, clean-room clock, inventory bag, Home Core latch, Memory
  Sea trace, waste class, landlord release, CHASER evidence line.
- Private tenderness: keeps a box of unclaimed door tags and calls them "names
  the city stopped needing."
- Anger pattern: "Do not ask me to clean a room that is still taking care of
  someone."
- Silence pattern: stops speaking when a Home Core routine addresses her by
  the wrong dead tenant's name.

## Beliefs

- Clean rooms are not always truthful rooms. Sometimes the mess is the only map
  of who was still being cared for.
- The Home Layer should not be reset on a landlord clock when a Home Core is
  still performing live care.
- Memory Bank calls preserved rooms assets, landlords call them delays, and
  sanitation calls them hazards. None of those labels answer whether the room
  witnessed a person.
- White Ghost Team is useful only if they arrive before the reset; after that,
  verification becomes a story about missing evidence.
- A worker should be allowed to say "leave this room dirty for eight hours"
  without becoming liable for every debt inside it.

## Description

Selene Arif is the person who arrives when a story is supposed to be over. Her
crew wears gray municipal scrub gear, carries sealed inventory bags, and has a
job nobody wants to think about: make the room usable again. In White Harbor,
usable can mean safe. It can also mean empty enough that nobody has to ask what
the Home Core was still doing.

Apartment 6C should be routine: eviction complete, source tenant dead, landlord
release active, Home Core scheduled for disconnect. Then the stove safety
routine refuses reset because it is still routing a dialysis reminder to the
neighbor through the Home Layer. The city sees a dirty room. Selene sees a
living dependency, a possible Ghost witness, a privacy breach, and an asset
freeze about to happen in the same eight hours.

She is not a hero and not a revolutionary. She is a contractor with bad lungs,
a supervisor badge, and one power the system forgot to make glamorous: she can
leave the room dirty long enough for someone else to tell the truth.

## Secret

Selene already recognizes the pattern because three years earlier she let a
similar room reset on time. The old room went clean, the neighbor missed two
medical reminders, and the discarded Home Core latch later appeared in a Black
Zone sale under a different family's grief label. She never proved the chain.
Apartment 6C gives her the same clock with a witness still alive.
