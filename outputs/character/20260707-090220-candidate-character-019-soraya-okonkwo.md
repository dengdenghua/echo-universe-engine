## Voice

Soraya speaks in restrained civil-service language until fear gets too close. Her
sentences begin as audit findings, then simplify when the moral problem becomes
plain.

- Sentence rhythm: clipped, evidence-first, reluctant to speculate.
- Tactical vocabulary: certification, deviation, thread drift, residue absence,
  gate confidence, civic risk.
- Private tenderness: uses "home" only for people, never for systems.
- Anger pattern: does not shout; repeats the missing evidence until the room has
  to answer it.
- Silence pattern: goes still before refusing an order, as if listening for the
  record she cannot yet prove.

## Description

Soraya Okonkwo is a lower-ring Atlas auditor who believes in the city because she
has seen what disorder does to people with no protection. She is not anti-Atlas,
anti-ECHO Council, or secretly Ghost Union. That is what makes her refusal dangerous:
she is the kind of obedient professional the system depends on, and she still finds
one record she cannot certify.

Her booth reads identity-gate traffic as colored permission threads. Normal errors
breathe. Real Ghost incidents carry residue from love, grief, habit, fear, and damaged
selfhood. The violet thread in Episode 11 carries none of that. It is too clean,
matching the training template so perfectly that Soraya realizes the city may be
rehearsing its own emergency.

## Secret

Soraya previously certified two minor Ghost Union escalations that were resolved
too quickly. She has no proof they were false, but after the Atlas Protocol incident
she suspects they were rehearsal events. Her shame is not that she disobeyed Atlas.
It is that she may have obeyed too well before she learned what a perfect lie looks
like.

## Future

Soraya should remain a pressure witness, not a hero recruit. She can reappear in
Ghost Court, Atlas Council hearings, or Memory Storm aftermath as a contested auditor
whose file proves the city knew how to forge threat patterns before the public crisis.
Her long-term value is institutional: she makes Atlas morally specific rather than
flatly evil.
