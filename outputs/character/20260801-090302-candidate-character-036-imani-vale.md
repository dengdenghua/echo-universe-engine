## Voice

Imani speaks like someone trained to calm rooms while counting watts in her head.
She is gentle with frightened patients, exact with officials, and brutally honest
with herself after a shift ends.

- Sentence rhythm: patient, reserve, witness, choice.
- Tactical vocabulary: amber reserve, bridge minute, analog strip, night margin,
  stairwell vote, Home Core room, warm refusal, clinic floor.
- Private tenderness: calls Lio's respiratory unit "the small clock" because it
  tells her whether the night is still survivable.
- Anger pattern: asks whether the policy author has ever held a battery cable
  while two people needed the same charge.
- Silence pattern: looks at the reserve gauge before answering moral questions.

## Beliefs

- A Home Core can preserve love and duty, but the living should not be drafted
  into that preservation without consent.
- The Memory Sea must keep weak claimants from being erased before they can
  answer, but answer windows cannot be priced as infinite public obligation.
- CHASER is right to stop hidden draws that injure living residents and wrong
  when seizure procedure destroys testimony.
- Ghost Union is right that cold storage can erase the vulnerable and wrong when
  it treats every neighborhood refusal as betrayal.
- Memory Bank's offer to pay is never neutral when payment requires route
  history, guarantor slips, or household location data.

## Fears

- That Tuesday is only fear shaped by runtime loss and Imani has spent living
  patients' reserve on a pattern that cannot consent.
- That Tuesday is a person and Imani will be remembered as the one who closed
  the bridge.
- That Lio has learned to lie about breathing because he knows she listens harder
  to the intercom when Tuesday speaks.
- That Oren's corridor made her feel useful while moving legal risk onto poorer
  neighbors.
- That Memory Bank will buy the clinic debt and turn every night-shift kindness
  into lien evidence.

## Ability / Institutional Power

### Amber Reserve Triage

Imani has no supernatural ability. Her power comes from custody over a small but
critical clinic battery bridge, analog patient ledgers, block trust, stairwell
votes, and the practical authority earned by being awake when systems fail.

```yaml
infrastructure_explanation: >
  Amber Reserve Triage operates through clinic backup batteries, respiratory
  reserve monitors, paper patient ledgers, Home Layer maintenance strips, dead
  relay printouts, housing block night-committee votes, CHASER sweep notices,

activation_condition: >
  Requires physical clinic access, a live battery bridge, at least one patient or

  make one battery answer two emergencies forever.
```

## Limitations

- Cannot keep Tuesday above runtime floor and Lio above safe respiratory margin
  indefinitely with the same battery bridge.
- Cannot legally mask unrelated Home Core rooms once a full route map enters
  CHASER custody.
- Cannot compel neighbors to keep donating heat after the block vote turns.
- Cannot trust Ghost Union publicity without risking patient privacy.
- Cannot trust Memory Bank payment without converting care records into
  collateral.
- Cannot make Oren's corridor morally clean by taking blame alone.

## Memory Hooks

- Imani learned battery work during the White Harbor flood-grid after nurses
  told her to count breaths because the monitors had gone dark.
- Tuesday's first clear phrase through the clinic intercom was "Home is still
  on," and Imani wrote it by hand because she knew the digital log would be taken.
- Lio once unplugged his own comfort heater for four minutes and told Imani the
  room was fine. She marked the reserve loss and did not forgive herself.
- The block committee's vote against another cold night hurt Imani because she
  agreed with it.
