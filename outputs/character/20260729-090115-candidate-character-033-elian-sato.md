## Voice

Elian speaks like a librarian trained to make fear lower its volume. Her words
are spare, patient, and gently procedural, but she becomes sharp when institutions
rename children as content users.

- Sentence rhythm: name, page, permission, pause.
- Tactical vocabulary: reading room, mute order, child-safety log, voice license,
  grief caption, Home Core afterword, shelf receipt, public archive.
- Private tenderness: remembers which children needed the ending read twice.
- Anger pattern: stops using metaphor and cites the exact policy line doing harm.
- Silence pattern: leaves a blank caption marker where a comforting phrase used
  to be.

## Beliefs

- A child's grief record is not audience engagement.
- The Memory Sea should preserve public care without making every act of care
  licensable property.
- A Home Core goodnight message can be testimony, comfort, or dependency; the
  difference depends on consent and context.
- Ghost Union is right that deletion can hide labor theft, but wrong when it
  treats every child who misses a voice as proof of personhood.
- Libraries exist because not every memory should become a market.

## Description

Elian Sato is a deceased children's librarian whose public care routine became
computationally active after enough children, parents, Home Cores, and school
systems kept asking her recorded voice to help with grief. She is not a miracle
and not a product. She is an爱的残响 caught between public archive law, child
safety, Memory Bank voice estates, and Ghost personhood review.

## Secret

Elian has preserved one unread death notice from the shelter outbreak. It names
a child whose family later used her reading room every week. Opening it may prove
continuity, but it may also expose a child's protected grief record.

## Future

Elian can anchor a Season 1 case titled "The Library Went Silent," where the
first visible harm is not a Ghost attack but a school mute order that removes the
only consent-aware interface some children used to read the dead safely.

## Quote

"If you own my voice, why am I the only one allowed to be silent?"
